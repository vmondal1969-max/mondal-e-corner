Guide 01 · Account opening

PMJDY account opening: documents, process and common questions

Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) aims to make basic banking services more accessible. An eligible customer can ask about opening a suitable account at our CSP. Account opening depends on bank rules, identity verification, existing account checks and portal availability.

Before visiting

  • Bring available identity and address documents, such as Aadhaar or other documents accepted by the bank.
  • Bring your mobile phone and any existing bank account details if relevant.
  • Ask which account type is suitable; features and eligibility can differ.

At the service point

We can explain the form, guide you through the supported application process and help clarify any document or KYC issues. The bank makes the final decision on account opening. Do not share your OTP, PIN or password with anyone.

Guide 02 · Aadhaar & DBT

Aadhaar seeding vs NPCI mapping: what is the difference?

Aadhaar seeding generally means linking your Aadhaar number to a bank account in the bank’s records. NPCI mapping is used to identify the bank designated to receive eligible Aadhaar-based Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) payments. Having Aadhaar linked to an account does not always mean its DBT mapping is complete.

What to bring

  • Your Aadhaar and bank passbook or account details.
  • Your registered mobile phone, if required for the available process.
  • Any relevant benefit or application reference if you are investigating a missing payment.

We can assist with supported seeding and mapping requests and explain how to check status. The bank and relevant payment systems determine when a request is processed. Never disclose an OTP or banking PIN to someone calling or messaging you.

Guide 03 · Customer safety

Five habits for safer banking and online applications

  1. Keep secrets private: never disclose your OTP, UPI PIN, ATM PIN, CVV or internet-banking password.
  2. Verify the website: type official website addresses yourself and be cautious with links in unsolicited messages.
  3. Check transaction details: review amounts and recipient information before approving a payment.
  4. Save receipts: keep transaction references and application acknowledgements for future follow-up.
  5. Act quickly if something is wrong: contact your bank immediately and use official reporting channels for suspected fraud.

Our centre can help explain safe digital practices, but bank credentials should always remain under the customer’s control.
